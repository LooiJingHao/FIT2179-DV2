Moving Malaysia

A Data Story of Our Growing Public Transport and its Challenges

Introduction

Malaysia's public transport system connects millions of commuters across rapidly growing urban regions. Expanding MRT, LRT, KTM, and bus networks have improved accessibility and urban connectivity, yet challenges involving congestion, unequal access, and sustainability continue shaping the future of Malaysia’s urban mobility landscape.

Act 2: The Geographic Reality

The Infrastructure Gap: Density vs. Raw Numbers

While demand is national, infrastructure is a story of concentration. This map reveals a geographic skew by showing public transport access points normalized by population (stations per 1 million residents). By looking at density rather than raw counts, we see that the Klang Valley isn't just better served—it's hyper-concentrated compared to the rest of the nation, creating friction as millions funnel into just a few high-density regions.

Act 3: The Gravity Centers

The Weight of the City: Mapping Malaysia's Hub Dynamics

Zooming into Malaysia's busiest region, we can map the "Gravity Centers" of our network. This Hub Mapping analysis plots Outbound (departures) against Inbound (arrivals) traffic.

Stations along the dashed diagonal line handle a balanced flow of commuters. However, those shifting toward the top-left (like TRX) act as "Sinks" where thousands funnel in for work, while bubbles toward the bottom-right are "Sources" of daily commuters. The size of each bubble represents the total weight of the station—revealing exactly where the system's gravitational pull is strongest.

Act 4: The Commuter Web

Systemic Friction: The Struggle of Shared Paths

Passenger flows are not just numbers; they are paths of least resistance. This matrix reveals that commuters aren't spreading out; they are essentially trying to occupy the same spaces at the same time. The dark cells highlight specific origin-destination pairs where demand is hyper-concentrated, creating intense friction in the daily urban commute.

Act 5: The Breaking Points

Evidence of Strain: Visualizing the Origin-Destination Flow

To understand the true limits of our growth, we must visualize how passengers move between major hubs. This Parallel Flow diagram connects origin stations (left) to their destinations (right). The thickness of the links represents the volume of daily commuters.

Notice the dominant corridors: the thickest flows center around Batu Caves and KL Sentral. By hovering over the links, the specific "strained" pathways are highlighted in red, proving that our network resilience is dictated by just a few high-volume connections that handle the majority of the weight.

Act 6: The Cultural Pulse

Beyond the Grind: The Rhythms of a Nation Reunited

Urban mobility isn't just about the home-to-work routine; it has a deeper, cultural heartbeat. By looking at 2025 seasonal behaviors, we see interstate friction. While urban lines face daily pressure, the ETS and Intercity corridors pulse during festive periods and holidays. This proves that transit is a vital cultural lifeline, moving families back to their roots across the nation during Malaysia's most important moments.

Act 7: Understanding Vulnerability

The Dependency Index: Which Stations Are Most At Risk?

As our network grows, some parts of the system become more "vulnerable" than others. The Dependency Index identifies stations that handle massive crowds but only have a single line to move them.

Stations like Bukit Bintang and KLCC process hundreds of thousands of people every day. Unlike major hubs with many lines, these stations don't have a "backup plan"—making them the most critical points in our daily commute.
